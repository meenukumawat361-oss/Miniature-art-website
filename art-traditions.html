    The Classical Schools of Indian Miniature Art

    Centuries of imperial durbars, sacred devotion, and Himalayan poetics brought alive through 24K Hilkari gold and single-hair squirrel brushwork.

    SCHOOL 01 • ROYAL HERITAGE

    Rajasthani Tradition: Kishangarh & Mewar

    Radha Bani Thani Mewar Durbar Kishangarh Poetics Natural Cinnabar & Gold

    The Rajasthani school represents the soul of Rajput chivalry, lyrical romance, and Krishna devotion. In Kishangarh, the legendary portrait of Radha Bani Thani—with exaggerated arched eyebrows, lotus-petal eyes, and a transparent odhna veil—remains India’s most celebrated archetype of divine beauty.

    Master Om Prakash Kumawat paints on antique Wasli with pure mineral vermilion and real Hilkari gold to ensure every nuance of royal jewelry and textile embroidery shines with celestial brilliance.

    View Rajasthani Artworks →
    SCHOOL 02 • IMPERIAL REALISM

    Mughal School: Court Realism & Hashiya

    Akbar & Jahangir Eras Intricate Hashiya Borders Single-Hair Realism Architectural Inlay

    Developed in imperial ateliers under emperors Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan, the Mughal school introduced photographic precision to Indian art. Intricate durbar ceremonies, lifelike portraits, exotic birds, and flora-fauna borders (*hashiya*) are executed with microscopic single squirrel-hair brushes.

    Our atelier preserves the authentic Mughal technique of multi-layered Wasli burnishing and lapis lazuli shading, creating historic assemblies that bridge East and West.

    View Mughal Artworks →
    SCHOOL 03 • HIMALAYAN LYRICISM

    Pahari School: Kangra & Basohli

    Kangra Valley Poetics Basohli Fiery Ochres Gita Govinda Verses Beetle-Wing Iridescence

    Sheltered in the verdant foothills of the Himalayas, Pahari miniatures are renowned for rhythmic line work, tender Radha-Krishna devotion, and lush monsoon foliage. From the fierce, jewel-like beetle-wing tones of Basohli to the lyrical, pastel serenity of Kangra, every painting is a sung verse of divine love.

    View Pahari Artworks →
    SCHOOL 04 • GOLCONDA SPLENDOR

    Deccani School: Golconda & Bijapur

    Deep Lapis Skies Persian Arabesques Heavy Gold Leaf Mystical Courtly Rhythms

    Flourishing in the wealthy Sultanates of Golconda and Bijapur, Deccani miniatures are distinguished by intense, luminous colors, sensual elegance, and starry midnight skies painted with pure Afghan lapis lazuli stone.

    View Deccani Artworks →
    SCHOOL 05 • MARTIAL GRANDEUR

    Sikh Heritage: Durbar of Maharaja Ranjit Singh

    Lahore Durbar Chronicles Gold Canopies & Regalia Monumental Compositions Historical Portraiture

    Commissioned during the 19th-century golden age of the Sikh Empire, this style celebrates the martial valor, courtly splendour, and diplomatic assembly of the Lahore kingdom under Sher-e-Punjab Maharaja Ranjit Singh.

    Master Om Prakash Kumawat spent five years completing his monumental Sikh Durbar canvas, which now resides in an elite international collection.

    View Sikh Durbar Artworks →
    SCHOOL 06 • ARCHAIC VIGOR

    Malwa & Central Indian Heritage

    Deep Chocolate & Red Grounds Rhythmic Epic Architecture Ramayana & Bhagavata Narratives Raw Earth Ochres

    Retaining an archaic boldness and geometric strength, Malwa miniatures feature deep chocolate-brown and lac-red backgrounds. Figures in vivid yellow and saffron robes enact epic narratives with dramatic emotional rhythm.
